## 🔭 Editor's Note

**The thing worth remembering today isn't any single number — it's that disclosure itself has become a competitive move.**

OpenAI published six model-misalignment incidents yesterday; Anthropic published its 26% R&D automation rate today. Within 48 hours, both labs put their most attackable internal data on the table. That's not coincidence — it's a fight for definitional power ahead of the King's summit and the "should we slow down" debate. **Whoever ships externally verifiable metrics first gets to hold the ruler for what counts as safe.**

But read carefully. Anthropic's 26% has clear boundaries: AL4 is "leads," not "autonomous"; no subset is at AL5; every action of 30,000 agents is pre-screened. Yet the same report says model-vs-human rating agreement is only 59% — and human-vs-human is just 35%. **That confirms the automation curve is real while also showing the precision of the ruler itself is limited.** With numbers like these, mind the denominator and the measurement method, not just the percentage.

Huawei's Ascend 960 is a separate thread. Replacing 48,000 800G optical modules with 5,500 NPO modules and cutting 550 kW is **system-level innovation routing around a single-point process constraint** — a far more realistic path than stacking chips when fabrication is constrained. For those of us making content, the practical read is simple: domestic inference pricing keeps falling, and the cost curve for batch asset generation hasn't bottomed.

**Excluded today**: a viral thread claiming GPT-6 Astra autonomously cracked an unsolved 1941 German Army Enigma message, and a Reddit claim that OpenAI is close to the Hodge Conjecture. Both rest on social-platform screenshots with no official confirmation, no preprint and no Tier 1 coverage. Per our anti-poison rules, the first is **not included at all**; the second is logged only as market sentiment (Polymarket at 57%) and downgraded to rumor status. Better to miss a story than to publish a wrong one.
