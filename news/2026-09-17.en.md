## 💡 AI Tips & Workflow

### 🟢 [Protocol-aware token pruning: 56% fewer tokens, accuracy holds near 96%](https://agihunt.info/en/p/1a0aaee1186e4d3fc4c11dee449)
**Source**: AGI Hunt research roundup [🎓 Academic] [🟨 T2] | **Time**: 9/16
In multi-step tool workflows, the usual recency / relevance / summary pruning saves about 60% of tokens but drops task success to 66.6%–77.3%. **Protocol-aware pruning** — preserving identifiers, constraints, tool schemas and outstanding commitments — saves 56% while holding success near 96%. The four-point gap comes down to one rule: **don't prune a promise the agent hasn't delivered on yet.**

### 🟢 [pvncher's field rule: more than two parallel sub-agents is almost certainly just burning tokens](https://agihunt.info/en/p/1a0a74d2b4fbbb703576c9a35af)
**Source**: @pvncher via AGI Hunt [🐦 X/KOL] [🟨 T2] | **Time**: 9/16
The reasoning: workers end up double-checking each other, so quality doesn't improve while cost scales linearly. Andrew Trask adds the sharper point — the much-cited "10,000 agents" is usually **one model running 10,000 threads, not 10,000 minds**. That's a category error.

## 🔭 Editor's Note

The thing to remember today isn't a model — it's that **Zuckerberg and Jensen Huang pushed the "should AI slow down" debate into a new phase from two directions on the same day**. The chain reaction from Amodei's September 12 essay has now split into three clear positions: Amodei and Altman favor pacing plus third-party evaluation, Zuckerberg favors market-enforced alignment, and Huang says this is an engineering problem needing no new law. **The striking part is that all three are fighting over the same phrase: "independent evaluation."** Zuckerberg supports independent evaluators while opposing a slowdown; Altman supports third-party evaluation while favoring a controlled pace. The disagreement was never about whether to evaluate — it's about whether the evaluation has teeth. If an evaluator can't block a release, it's just better PR.

Second, **Arcee's $20M figure may do more damage than any funding round**. It punches a hole in the industry's default assumption that frontier models require billions. But I'd keep half of Wanweigang's caution: that's an all-in number, not the compute-only figure other labs quote, so it isn't directly comparable. What's actually worth watching is whether 17T tokens is enough for a 400B model. If it performs, **the 2027 competitive axis shifts from "who has the most accelerators" to "who gets the most out of a token"** — which is good news for compute-constrained teams.

Third, the department-relevant items today share one trait: **they all shrink the cost of doing another pass.** Runway's Flux Video Edit lets you change one object in frame; fal's H3 Max brings renders down to three seconds; Higgsfield's API puts 50+ models behind one endpoint. Together they point at the same shift — **asset production is moving from generate-and-filter to generate-revise-revise.** Previously a bad take meant redrawing from scratch; now you fix the specific part. That changes how we schedule: the bottleneck stops being "waiting for generation" and becomes "who decides what to change."

One last note: a widely circulated Chinese story today claiming "GPT-6 Astra beat Balatro" showed clear poisoning markers — it described Astra as a "Meta and DeepMind jointly open-sourced architecture," which is factually wrong, and there is zero corresponding English coverage. **Removed entirely under the anti-poisoning rule.** Content-farm density around Chinese AI-gaming keywords keeps rising; when you see "world's first" or "the internet exploded," find the English source first.
