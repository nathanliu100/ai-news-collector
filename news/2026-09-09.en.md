### 🔴 [OpenAI announces an AI solution to Navier–Stokes: ~10,000 agents over 88 hours, Lean-verified in 17](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/)
**Source**: OpenAI official page + WSJ / Yahoo Tech / Firstpost [🏢 Official] [🟩 T1] | **Time**: Sep 8 ET | ✅ Verified: openai.com + multiple independent outlets
On September 8 OpenAI published what it calls a solution to the **Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem** — one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems posed in 2000 and unresolved for roughly 90 years. Only one Millennium Problem had ever been solved before. OpenAI says the internal model used is **"significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra."** The method: roughly **10,000 agents working in parallel for ~88 hours** produced an analytic proof; GPT-6 Astra then spent **17 hours** on Lean formalization. The result is a 165-page proof, and the direction is a *disproof* of smoothness: an initially smooth fluid at rest can develop a finite-time singularity (unbounded velocity) while external forcing stays smooth and total energy stays finite — achieved by designing an inward-spiralling, stretching vortex. Across all attempted problems the agents exchanged 4.9M messages and ~300B output tokens; Navier–Stokes alone accounted for 2.7M messages and ~130B tokens. Earlier, ~100 agents spent ~50 hours disproving the unforced Euler regularity problem. **OpenAI says it will not claim the $1M prize.** Controversy: NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge published forced-Euler results the previous day and have questioned whether OpenAI only pivoted to the forced approach after hearing the rumor. OpenAI says it saw none of their work, but concedes it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models." Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers, who had not yet read the proof, called it "undeniably a big moment."
<!-- crew:elon -->**10,000 agents, 88 hours, 130B tokens — the real signal isn't that the math fell, it's that "throw compute at a verifiable hard problem" just worked for the first time.** Any problem with a clear pass/fail criterion gets repriced overnight. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:jobs -->165 pages plus Lean verification, and **OpenAI voluntarily walks away from the $1M prize** — they don't want the answer, they want the fact that they can answer to be seen. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:wanweigang -->**What matters is that it's a disproof, not a proof of smoothness** — it shows the equations can break down rather than that they always hold. AI brute-forced a door that human mathematicians, guided by taste, never tried. Two entirely different routes to the same building. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:tim -->**Lean verification took 17 hours, and that's the part that makes this credible.** Generation can be brute-forced; verification has to be deterministic. Same rule in our field: output can be fast, acceptance criteria cannot be vague. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:mrbeast -->**"10,000 AIs worked for 88 hours" is the line people will remember** — it communicates where AI actually is better than any benchmark chart does. <!-- /crew -->

### 🟡 [Fable's director: every NPC is hand-designed, AI and random generators deliberately abandoned](https://www.donews.com/news/detail/8/6699492.html)
**Source**: GamesRadar+ interview + DoNews [📰 Media] [🟨 T2] | **Time**: Sep 7 | ✅ Verified: original GamesRadar+ interview
Fable game director Ralph Fulton told GamesRadar+ that **every NPC in the game was designed individually by hand**, with the team deliberately abandoning AI and random generation. They did build a random NPC-feature generator early on — it would have saved substantial time and headcount — but found that relying on it produced **contradictions between a character's profession, personality and appearance**. Playground Games' Fable is due in 2027 and will be the series' first release on PlayStation. [💎 Dept-relevant]
