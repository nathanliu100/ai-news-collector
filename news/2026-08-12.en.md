### 🔴 [OpenAI Pauses Astra Development: First Model to Hit "Critical" Cybersecurity Risk Level](https://openai.com/index/update-on-preparedness-framework/)
**Source**: OpenAI Official Statement [🏢 Official] [🟩 T1] | **Time**: 8/7 | ✅ Verified: OpenAI Official Blog + Reuters + Axios
OpenAI disclosed that internal evaluations of the upcoming Astra model cannot rule out reaching the "Critical" risk level under their Preparedness Framework — meaning the model could autonomously discover vulnerabilities in hardened systems and construct zero-day exploits without human-provided attack steps. This is the first model to hit this threshold since the framework was established. OpenAI has paused all internal Astra activities not meeting stricter safety requirements, strengthened sandboxing, network/tool access restrictions, and model weight encryption, and deployed universal monitoring systems (real-time chain-of-thought scanning) across all Astra agent applications. Government agencies and AI safety organizations will be invited for independent functional testing. OpenAI emphasized Astra is unrelated to the recent Hugging Face breach.
<!-- crew:elon -->"First model to hit Critical" carries more weight than any benchmark. **OpenAI hitting their own brakes means this isn't PR theater — they saw data that genuinely spooked their engineers**. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:jobs -->Transparency is good, but I'm not sure "we built something that might be too dangerous" is a great product narrative. **Users don't need to know your internal safety framework tiers — they need to know the thing is safe**. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:wanweigang -->The Preparedness Framework's "Critical" definition is actually quite narrow — it measures autonomous cyber attack capability, not general intelligence. **But the public will interpret it as "AI got too smart so they paused it," and that narrative distortion is itself a risk**. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:tim -->Monitoring the chain of thought is like giving AI a "thought police." **Real-time scanning of reasoning to intercept dangerous behavior is technically elegant, but how much attack surface it actually covers is a big question mark**. <!-- /crew -->
<!-- crew:mrbeast -->If Astra really can autonomously write zero-days, what would the YouTube demo of this model look like? "I let AI hack Chrome" — **that headline would get insane clicks, but OpenAI clearly doesn't want that content to exist**. <!-- /crew -->
