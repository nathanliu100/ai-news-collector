### 🟡 [Tencent's Gander: a voice agent that keeps talking while it works](https://the-decoder.com/tencents-gander-aims-to-keep-talking-while-it-works-in-the-background/)
**Source**: The Decoder [📰 Media] [🟨 T2] | **Time**: arXiv Sep 8, revised Sep 12 | ⚠️ No official Tencent announcement page found
Tencent's Hunyuan Speech team and academic collaborators introduce Gander, which splits real-time conversation from background work. The "cerebellum" handling conversation is a 9B model built on MiniCPM-o 4.5; it slices input into one-second windows and predicts one control token per window — listen, speak, or interrupt itself. The "brain" doing reasoning and execution is hot-swappable: Codex or Claude Code can drop in with no retraining of the conversation side. In the authors' runs it was an undisclosed GPT-5.6 family model. On Full-Duplex-Bench v3, Gander takes the floor at the right moment in all 100 scenarios and interrupts users 8.0% of the time, against 13.5% for GPT-Realtime and 47.9% for the weakest system. The cost is accuracy: strict Pass@1 is 0.400 versus 0.600 for GPT-Realtime, and it lands last on all four task-accuracy metrics. It also opens 51.6% of turns with a conversational placeholder. Code is on GitHub under Apache 2.0; weights and training data are pending the open-source release process. [💎 Dept relevant]

### 🔴 [Runway reveals real-time video generation research: from prompt-and-wait to a stream you steer](https://the-decoder.com/runway-wants-to-turn-ai-video-generation-into-a-live-stream-you-control-in-real-time/)
**Source**: Runway official research [🏢 Official] [🟩 T1] | **Time**: Sep 21 | ✅ Verified: Runway research page + independent coverage from The Decoder and ai-issue
Runway is attacking wait time, not image quality: users consistently report that most of their time goes to generating and revising. The approach builds on GWM-1, its first General World Model released in December 2025, which accepts camera movement, robot commands or audio as control input, plus Gen-4.5, using autoregressive causal diffusion where each new segment conditions on all previously generated frames. The central problem is error accumulation: a text model can correct itself mid-sentence, but a video model builds every frame on the previous one, so small errors compound into major distortion. Runway's answer is to train on **its own flawed outputs** so the model learns to recover rather than amplify deviation — Decart used a comparable technique for MirageLSD. Runway showed a real-time model with NVIDIA at GTC in March, running on the Vera Rubin platform with a sub-100ms first-frame target. Long-term use cases named are education, gaming and robotics. No availability date announced. [💎 Dept relevant] [🎯 Focus tool]

## 🔭 Editor's Notes

**1. Today's most important story is not a model, it is a door.** Amazon closed one and Shopify opened one, on the same day, about the same agent. The significance reaches past commerce: it is the first time the question "does a third-party agent have the right to operate someone else's service on a user's behalf" has been put on the table. Amazon's wording is telling — it does not say "we refuse," it says third parties should operate openly and respect the service provider's decision about whether to participate. If that phrasing becomes the industry norm, every agent will need to carry an ID card. **The coming agent competition may be less about who can do more and more about whose identity more platforms accept.**

**2. Grok 4.7's numbers and its narrative are on different channels.** The official framing — bigger base, longer RL, same price — reads as a solid upgrade. But in a community test with 105 planted bugs across two real repos, find-and-fix scored Grok 4.7 xHigh at 28.7, identical to 4.6. Terminal-Bench jumping from 20.3% to 38.0% is a genuine gain, yet next to Fable 5.1 Max's 57.9% it still sits well behind. **This is not an argument that Grok 4.7 is bad; it is that the gap between official benchmarks and real workflows is uncomfortably wide today.** We report official figures and the third-party measurements alongside them for exactly this reason.

**3. Anti-poisoning: three items were held back today.** ① "A model trained for 24 days solved 100+ long-standing open math problems" — a Reddit post only, with no paper, official or institutional confirmation, and the OpenAI announcement it points to is about forming a math and AI advisory group, not a results list. **Not included.** ② "DeepSeek is training a 2T model with 8T on the roadmap" — online rumor only, zero official confirmation. **Not included.** ③ A string of unconfirmed calendar items — "Gemini 4.0 in staged testing," "Anthropic Opus 5.5 in stealth development" — sourced from second-hand quotes and video roundups. **No standalone entries.** Better to miss than to misreport; that rule got used three times today.

**4. One number worth filing separately: the 133-patent-attorney randomized trial.** AI-assisted drafting lifted junior quality by 0.58–0.60 standard deviations, which looks like a rout. But when AI was removed for judgment-heavy revision work, **junior gains went to zero while seniors retained 0.45 SD**. AI raises this run's output, not the person's capability. For any organization hoping to use AI as a training tool, that is a blunt warning: **borrowed capability disappears the moment the tool is taken away.**

Editorial process note.** Yesterday (Sep 21) already carried Anthropic's Bay Area wet lab, Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash and the Qwen-Image-2.1 launch; today covers only follow-ups and ecosystem developments, with no repeated slots.
